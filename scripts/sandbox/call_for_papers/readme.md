Title: Passive Acoustic Sensing Reveals Context-Dependent Expression of Personality Pathology: A Bayesian Multilevel Ambulatory Assessment Study

Background: Personality pathology is theorized to be context-dependent, yet empirical methods for capturing dynamic trait expression in naturalistic settings remain underdeveloped. Traditional ecological momentary assessment relies on self-report, which may be biased or incomplete. We introduce passive acoustic analysis as an objective, scalable approach to assess how real-world stressors modulate maladaptive trait expression.

Methods: In 140 university students, we administered the full PID-5 at baseline, then brief weekly EMA assessments using 15 items (3 per domain, factor-analytically selected from >1,000 participants). Participants recorded vocal samples (sustained vowels /a/, /i/, /u/; sentence reading) at baseline (distant from exam), pre-exam, and post-exam. We extracted acoustic features indexing physiological arousal (fundamental frequency, formants) and vocal quality (jitter, noise-to-harmonics ratio). Bayesian multilevel models with weakly informative priors tested how each PID-5 domain moderated stress reactivity and recovery across six acoustic outcomes.

Results: Negative Affectivity amplified stress-induced pitch increases (β=5.03 Hz [1.26,8.76]). Detachment impaired post-stress recovery (β=-4.37 [-8.30,-0.46]), while Antagonism facilitated it (β=3.44-4.17). Psychoticism showed pervasive pitch elevation and voice quality degradation. Disinhibition exhibited domain-specific articulatory alterations (F2: β=63 Hz [26,99]), suggesting stable motor dyscontrol. Formant features showed particularly large moderation effects (β=63-117 Hz).

Conclusions: Passive acoustic sensing captured theoretically-predicted patterns of context-dependent personality pathology: some traits amplify stress responses dynamically (NA, Detachment, Antagonism), while others leave stable signatures on vocal production (Psychoticism, Disinhibition). These findings provide further evidence for the construct validity of maladaptive trait dimensions; moreover this objective, continuous, ecologically-valid approach complements self-report ambulatory assessment.
